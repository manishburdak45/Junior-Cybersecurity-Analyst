# History of Networking

From early research and the formal proposal of ARPANET, to the first connection, its growth, and the rise of private networks.

01 / Beginning of Networking

## From research to a working connection

**1959**ARPA

**1960–1966**Theoretical research

**1966**ARPANET formally proposed

**1968**BBN tasked to design interfacing processors

**October 29, 1969**First ARPANET connection between UCLA and SRI

The order shows the path: organization, theory, formal proposal, design of the interfacing hardware, and finally a real connection.

**Additional Understanding.** Many references give 1958 for the establishment of ARPA. For answers that follow the lecture, use 1959.

02 / ARPANET

## The first connection

UCLA

SRI

October 29, 1969

The first ARPANET connection joined UCLA and SRI. Two sites and one link is the simplest possible network, and ARPANET began at this scale.

**Additional Understanding.** The lecture points used here do not state why ARPANET was developed. In general history it was built so that research computers at different sites could share resources, and it is widely regarded as a predecessor of the Internet.

**1973 — International connection.** First international connections, to Norway and the UK. The lecture gives no node count for 1973.

The network grew from 4 nodes in 1969 to 23 nodes in 1972, more than five times as many in three years. The number of nodes rose every year, and in 1973 the network extended beyond one country.
